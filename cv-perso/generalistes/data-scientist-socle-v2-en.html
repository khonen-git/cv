Selected ML & Statistical Research

Four lines of experimentation on regimes, predictability, and event dynamics: frame a question, set up out-of-sample validation, then measure what remains informative once context and artefacts are controlled. The goal is not to accumulate scores, but to separate statistical structure from genuinely actionable use.

01 — Dual-horizon regimes & competing outcomes

HMM · competing risks · OOS

Question
When the short-horizon regime turns neutral while the long-horizon regime stays directional, what happens next?

Methods
  • Two HMMs (different horizons), states −1 / 0 / +1; causal OOS filtering.
  • After a short-regime pause (fast 1→0, slow still directional): three mutually exclusive outcomes — resume long direction (Resolve), short-term flip (Escalate), or long-regime break first (Break). Symmetric bull / bear.
Finding
~28% Resolve
~68% Escalate
~4% Break

After the pause, short-term flips dominate resumes in the long-regime direction; long-regime breaks are rare (~4%).

02 — Predictability of market regimes

RF · XGBoost · event conditioning

Question
Can amplitude-based regime labels at time T be predicted exploitably OOS — globally and conditional on events?

Methods
  • Amplitude-based regime labeling; RF / XGBoost vs baselines; purged / walk-forward temporal validation.
  • Global evaluation vs conditioning on heterogeneous events (microstructure breaks, volatility expansion / compression, crosses, etc.).
Finding
Global: ROC-AUC ≈ 0.70–0.80 — informative signal, often on regime endings Event-conditional: sharp degradation → near chance

Statistically present signal, but little edge: regime endings are not always actionable, and conditional on events the advantage disappears.

03 — HMM denoising & state persistence

HMM · Z-scores · transitions

Question
Does HMM smoothing of derived signals reveal usable persistence / state transitions?

Methods
  • HMM denoising of Z-scores and derivatives (median Z-score, position in high–low range, etc.).
  • Analysis of state persistence and Markovian transitions.
Finding

Clear persistence and Markovian structure — enough statistical information, no direct trading edge. Lead: use these states as event conditions rather than a standalone signal.

04 — Information lifetime after events

Survival · event sampling · hazard

Question
After an event, under what law does information decay — and at what horizon does it stop being detectable?

Methods
  • Event sampling then survival / first-passage analysis on effect lifetime.
  • Calibration of hazard-type laws (exponential / Poisson-process form) to describe decay.
Finding

Promising frame for treating an effect as a temporal process rather than a point score; on this setup, no stable edge yet — value is mainly methodological (useful horizon, survival law).
